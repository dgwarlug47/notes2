# *Alea Iacta Est*

## Crossing the Rubicon

- [Cause] [Caesar crossed the Rubicon because returning without protection exposed him to prosecution]
  - [Negotiation] [Caesar sought a political office that would preserve legal immunity]
  - [Breakdown] [The Senate rejected the arrangement and left him no acceptable alternative]
- [Event] [In January 49 BCE Caesar led Legio XIII from Cisalpine Gaul across the Rubicon into Italy] The act violated the law governing *imperium* and made armed conflict inevitable.
- [Meaning] [Crossing the Rubicon means passing the point of no return]
- [Optimates' response] [Pompey and many senators fled south because they overestimated Caesar's advancing force]
- [Immediate result] [Caesar captured Rome without resistance]

## Battle of Ilerda

- [Strategic objective] [Caesar sought to neutralize Pompey's stronghold in Hispania]
- [Pompeian base] [Pompey held Hispania as a major provincial base with many legions]
- [Pompeian advantage] [Afranius and Petreius commanded five legions from strong ground near Ilerda] They controlled high ground and vital supplies.

## Battle of Pharsalus

- [Strategic objective] [Caesar pursued Pompey into Greece before Pompeian strength could grow]
- [Significance] [Pharsalus became the decisive battle of the Caesarian civil war]
- [Crisis] [Caesar's situation became critical because time favored Pompey]
- [Forces] [Caesar fielded roughly 30,000 veteran infantry and 2,000 cavalry]
- [Forces] [Pompey fielded roughly 47,000 infantry and 7,000 cavalry with positional superiority]
- [Qualification] [The headline numbers obscure differences in combat experience]
- [Pompeian strategy] [Pompey preferred Fabian delay and supply interdiction]
- [Caesarian strategy] [Caesar needed decisive battle because delay favored Pompey]
- [Position] [Pompey's army waited uphill while Caesar occupied lower ground]
- [Initiative] [Pompey initially refused battle, leading Caesar to feign departure]
- [Political pressure] [Confident Optimates pushed Pompey toward engagement]

### Battle Sequence

- [Tactic] [Pompey ordered his line to remain still so Caesar's charging infantry would tire]
- [Discipline] [Caesar's veterans recognized the tactic and paused before resuming their advance]
- [Decisive reserve] [Six concealed cohorts were trained to counter Pompey's cavalry]
- [Cavalry action] [Labienus drove back Caesar's cavalry on Pompey's left]
- [Surprise] [Caesar revealed a fourth infantry line and ordered pila to be thrust rather than thrown]
- [Result] [Pompey's cavalry panicked, suffered heavy casualties, and exposed the flank]

### Pompey's Retreat and Death

- [Collapse] [Pompey's retreat caused his legions to panic and flee]
- [Camp] [Pompey expected a long battle but soon learned Caesar's troops were entering his camp]
- [Flight] [Pompey escaped in disguise and sought new support in Egypt]
- [Assassination] [Egyptian authorities killed Pompey in an attempt to please Caesar]
- [Caesar's response] [Caesar wept when presented with Pompey's head] Pompey had been his longtime ally and son-in-law, and Caesar had intended to pardon him.

## Battle of Zela

- [Event] [Caesar defeated Pharnaces II near Zela in 47 BCE]
- [Summary] [*Veni, vidi, vici* compresses the speed and completeness of the victory]
- [Pharnaces' strategy] [Pharnaces delayed because Caesar faced many competing crises]
- [Battle] [Pharnaces unexpectedly crossed the valley and attacked uphill while Caesar's camp was unfinished]
- [Defense] [Roman pila stopped the scythed chariots that penetrated Caesar's thin line]
- [Counterattack] [Caesar drove the Pontic army downhill, routed it, and captured its camp]

# Caesar's Victory

## Destruction of the Optimates

- [Result] [Caesar destroyed much of Rome's oldest patrician elite]

## Four Triumphs

- [Ritual] [Triumphs displayed battle paintings, spoils, and prominent prisoners]
- [Gallic triumph] [The conquest of Gaul doubled Roman territory and ended with the execution of the Gallic king]
- [Egyptian triumph] [The Egyptian celebration was not a conventional territorial triumph]
- [Pontic triumph] [The Pontic celebration did not commemorate permanent annexation]
- [African triumph] [Romans objected to images showing Optimates dying]
- [Reward] [Each soldier received one silver talent, roughly fifteen years of military pay]
- [Public distribution] [Roman citizens received roughly four years of minimum wages]
- [Senate] [Caesar expanded the Senate from about 300 to 1,000 members]
- [Calendar reform] [Caesar established a twelve-month calendar] July and August were added in the inherited naming scheme, while December still preserves the Latin root for ten.

# Death of Julius Caesar

## Public Excess

- [Crown test] [Caesar had Mark Antony offer him a crown publicly to test the crowd]
- [Golden throne] [A golden Senate throne appeared extravagantly monarchical]
- [Tribunes] [Caesar imprisoned a tribune who opposed public royal symbolism]

## Root Cause

- [Strategic error] [Caesar pardoned enemies who later organized his assassination]
- [Conspirators] [Marcus Junius Brutus and Gaius Cassius led the plot]

## Motives

- [Urgency] [The conspirators acted before Caesar departed on further eastern campaigns]
- [Dictatorship] [His title of dictator for life signaled the Republic's loss of power]
- [Mercy paradox] [The enemies able to kill Caesar remained alive because he had pardoned them]

## Ides of March

- [Warnings] [A priest advised Caesar to stay home and his wife reported a nightmare]
- [Condition] [Caesar was hungover]
- [Persuasion] [Brutus argued that Caesar could not summon the Senate and then fail to attend]

## Succession

- [Antony] [Mark Antony's poor governance of Italy weakened his position as Caesar's principal heir]
- [Octavian] [Campaign experience in Spain revealed Octavian's intelligence]
- [Heir] [Caesar selected the eighteen-year-old Octavian partly because he lacked a son]
